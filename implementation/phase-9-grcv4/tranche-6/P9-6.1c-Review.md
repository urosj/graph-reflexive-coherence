## Review of the analytic residual bridge

Let the represented finite input values denote exact real operands. The
analytic residual evaluator reconstructs the constitutive equations before
output rounding. At the returned binary64 pair it encloses each exact component
in `[l_i,u_i]`. Therefore

`||F(J,h)||_2^2 <= sum_i max(|l_i|,|u_i|)^2 = residual_squared`.

Both current and geometry entries contribute; geometry uses all matrix entries,
consistent with its Frobenius norm. The comparison to the declared absolute
tolerance squared is rational and exact. The numerical current/structural-source
diagnostics are not the admission criterion. This proves an absolute residual
bound for an approximation to the certified local root; it does not claim an
exact representable root, a global solution error bound or arbitrary-seed
convergence. The enclosure may fail conservatively even when a real root exists.

The arithmetic cases were checked as follows:

* Interval addition and multiplication use endpoint extrema. Division first
  excludes zero, then uses reciprocal endpoints, including negative intervals.
  Every nonpoint result rounds its lower endpoint down and upper endpoint up
  on a scale-relative dyadic grid. Exact point operations remain rational.
  Cancellation can widen a bound but cannot erase a defect.
* Exponential range reduction uses `exp(q)=exp(q/2^s)^(2^s)` with reduced
  argument at most 1/2. After Taylor degree 32, the first omitted term is t33;
  all successive ratios are at most x/34, so `t33/(1-x/34)` bounds the tail.
  Positive squaring and negative-argument reciprocals preserve enclosure.
  The A floor shortcut below -1000 is below the smallest positive binary64
  floor. The finite evaluation budget is an explicit conservative admission.
* Tanh is evaluated through its real exponential identity and monotonicity.
  Beyond magnitude 512 the endpoint is enclosed between tanh(512) and 1,
  with the negative case obtained by oddness. Rounded saturation is not assumed
  equal to the analytic value. Square-root bounds likewise enclose the real
  star-overlap coefficient rather than the platform square-root output.
* A's baseline interval is a point because it uses only exact rational linear
  operations. Reading its lower endpoint to form the exponent therefore does
  not discard uncertainty. The declared differential normal systems and
  mobility products are unrounded in both the residual and domain certificate.

For an interval matrix A, the midpoint inverse M is computed rationally.
The interval product bounds `q=||I-M A||_infinity`. If q<1, every represented A
is invertible and

`||A^-1-M||_infinity <= q*||M||_infinity/(1-q)`.

This follows by writing `A^-1=(I-(I-M A))^-1 M` and bounding the Neumann tail.
The induced infinity norm bounds every entry, which justifies the returned
entry intervals even for nonsymmetric matrices. A singular midpoint or q>=1
fails with `no_admitted_root`. New exact rational pressure covers q approaching
one, the singular boundary, and exact-point inverses.

## Review of the C spectral and coordinate bounds

The nontrivial projector enclosure uses numerical columns only to suggest a
subspace. Rational orthogonalization builds an exact orthoprojector E. With
Q=I-E, exact inertia verifies that EAE is below the cutoff on range(E) and QAQ
is above it on range(Q). The auxiliary identity blocks make these full-matrix
positive-definiteness checks without changing either compressed sign.

Write `S=EAE+QAQ`. If `g=1/upper_norm((S-Lambda I)^-1)` and
`e>=||A-S||_F` satisfy e<g, the entire S-to-A segment has strict cutoff gap
at least g-e. E is the below-cutoff projector of S. If P is that of A, the
two cross-subspace Sylvester equations give

`||(I-P)E||_F, ||P(I-E)||_F <= e/(2g-e)`.

Consequently `||P-E||_F <= sqrt(2)*e/(2g-e) <= 2e/(g-e)`, the conservative
runtime bound. The Frobenius residual e supplies the dimension dependence;
there is no omitted factor from converting an operator residual to this norm.
Each projector entry is therefore enclosed. The kernel shortcut follows
from SPD H1 and unit H0; its component-constant projector is exact. Empty/full
selected subspaces are also exact, using the independently established rank.

The new tilted-subspace test matters here: a slightly wrong subspace can pass
both compressed signs. The error enclosure must still cover the true projector.
Three tilt scales pass and enclose the known rational path-graph projector;
the earlier grossly wrong-subspace rejection is reused.

For the whole-ball contraction calculation, intermediate H_M, Q, resolvent
and inverse derivatives are bounded in the **induced operator norm**, with
Frobenius h-perturbations as input. In particular,
`||dH_M||_2 <= d^2 ||dh||_F + 2 d ||H||_2 ||dD||_2`.
The per-edge deformation bound controls the diagonal operator norm of dD;
