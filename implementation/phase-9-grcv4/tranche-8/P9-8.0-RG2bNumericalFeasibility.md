## Finite graph-transform chain

`G(X,H)=I+kappa_H S(PX,H)`, with exactly the frozen argument retraction:
resources to `[-1,5]`, and A scaled log-history `Y=512 log W` to `[-1,1]`.
The state itself and the update output are not projected. Signed exterior
queries are auxiliary only. The same completion applies at **every** depth.

Let `Gamma_0=I` and `Gamma_N=T^N Gamma_0`, for the accepted graph transform T.
At query `x_0`, a length-N chain satisfies

$$
x_i+f(Px_i,H_i)=x_{i-1},\qquad
H_{i-1}=I+\kappa_H S(Px_i,H_i),\quad 1\le i\le N,
\qquad H_N=I.
$$

Successive use of the accepted global inverse gives
`H_i=Gamma_(N-i)(x_i)` and hence `H_0=Gamma_N(x_0)`.
This is the nested graph transform evaluated as a finite coupled system; it
is not an instantaneous H root or an OS predictor. Each graph has its own
chain and section. No source section is transported as target history.

The represented algorithm initializes all x entries at the query and all H
entries at identity. Each of 18 sweeps updates states in increasing depth by
`x_i <- x_(i-1)-f(Px_i,H_i)`, then geometries in decreasing depth by the source
equation. Use **N=4 for A and N=6 for C**. Eighteen sweeps are a reproducible
algorithm choice, not a residual or convergence proof: every returned chain
must independently pass the certificate below. No arbitrary-depth backward
containment assumption or physical-tube restriction enters the argument.

## Residual, inversion and truncation certificate

Use the accepted global bounds `A_X,A_H` for f and `B_X,B_H` for S, in state
max norm and geometry induced-infinity norm. The verifier recomputes, over
every coordinate at every depth,

$$
r_X=\max_i\|\widehat x_i+f(P\widehat x_i,\widehat H_i)
                  -\widehat x_{i-1}\|_\infty,\qquad
r_H=\max_i\|\widehat H_{i-1}-I-\kappa_H
                         S(P\widehat x_i,\widehat H_i)\|_\infty.
$$

It independently assembles the frozen clamp using exact dyadic inputs and
literal signed interval equations. It does not call either producer's
`increment()` or the interval clipping helper. All represented H matrices must
be finite, symmetric, star-supported and inside the accepted `rho=2^-12`
ball, and the terminal H must be exactly I. Shape, complete history coordinates,
graph-size/degree/Gram/mask bounds, connectivity and parameters are checked.

For the exact finite chain with the same represented query `x_0`, let E_X and
E_H bound the maximum state and geometry errors. Inverse subtraction yields

$$
e_{X,i}\le\frac{e_{X,i-1}+r_X+A_HE_H}{1-A_X},\qquad
D_N=\sum_{j=1}^N(1-A_X)^{-j},\qquad
E_X\le D_N(r_X+A_HE_H).
$$

The source equations and exact terminal geometry give

$$
E_H\le r_H+\kappa_H B_X E_X+\kappa_H B_H E_H.
$$

Consequently the rational quantities used by the checker are

$$
\Delta_N=1-\kappa_H B_H-\kappa_H B_XD_NA_H>0,
\qquad
\overline E_H=\frac{r_H+\kappa_H B_XD_Nr_X}{\Delta_N},
\qquad
\overline E_X=D_N(r_X+A_H\overline E_H).
$$

For A4 and C6, the denominators exceed 0.999925 and 0.997762 respectively.
These estimates compare with an existing exact chain supplied by the global
inverse/section theorem; there is no circular assumption that the numerical
iteration has converged. A bad chain simply has larger recomputed residuals
and fails its budget.

The accepted section contraction q and value bound V=`kappa_H M_S` imply

$$
\|\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\sup}\le q^N V.
$$

This uses `||Gamma_0-Gamma|| <= V`, so no extra `1/(1-q)` factor is needed.
Let e_in enclose conversion from the intended query to represented x_0,
including all A log-history coordinates. With the accepted Lipschitz bound
L=`2^-10`, the full section output error is

$$
E_{\rm section}=\overline E_H+q^NV+L e_{\rm in}<2^{-48}.
$$

To enclose the exact H supplied to the physical current, center the interval
on the independently reevaluated first source image
`I+kappa_H S(P xhat_1,Hhat_1)`, with padding

$$
\kappa_H(B_X\overline E_X+B_H\overline E_H)+q^NV+L e_{\rm in}.
$$

Disjoint-edge entries stay exactly zero. This avoids falsely treating rounded
identity-diagonal publication errors as independent errors on every source
entry. The separate output bound above still includes the actual represented
