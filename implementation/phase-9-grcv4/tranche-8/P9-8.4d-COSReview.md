The event-supplied simplex is an explicit fixture input, not an autonomous
resource allocation law. D59/66/73 use (22,21,21)/64 in branch order. D94
uses (17,24,23)/64 starting at the deeper remainder branch and following
chirality. These new tuples receive new event identities. Old records are
not relabeled or reused as evidence for the changed requests.

The uncoupled identity-Hodge design diagnostic is the signed L^2 resource
increment. A larger branch degree tightens the zero-node inequalities. Merely
extending the previous (1/4,1/2,1/4) tuple fails current-role positivity;
near-equal shares cure that first-step problem but fail the second step on
the depth-four target. Lowering the deep branch's share yields the useful
real-simplex candidate (1/4,3/8,3/8), but its represented products gain
exactly 2^-53 charge on the actual current source (reset remains exact).
The declared (17,24,23)/64 successor keeps both represented role sums exact
and passes the full enabled-law ten-step dense screen. These diagnostics
informed fixture selection before native execution; they are not a general
positivity theorem. Tests preserve all three exclusions without incomplete
trajectory dumps or a changed numerical tolerance.

The source's C law is not claimed to generate a positive semigroup for all
states or graphs. In particular, depth-four first-step resources near 1e-14
need native admission/positivity checks; the 2e-13 dense comparison ceiling
alone cannot certify their signs. A bounded native pass would not prove a
