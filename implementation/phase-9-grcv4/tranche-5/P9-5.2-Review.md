## Numerical contract and pressure

The potential's finite binary64 operands are combined rationally, then its
combined components round once. `reference_potential_exact` and
`geometry_potential_increment_exact` retain immutable `Fraction` tuples in
the input graph's live-node order. These diagnostics can exceed binary64 range;
they neither impose separate range admission nor enter authoritative payloads.
Input-only roundtrips recompute them exactly. The combined potential, baseline
and current still require finite binary64 values. The baseline uses the already
validated/rounded A mobility factor and rounded potential, with an exact contraction before its
own rounding. Fresh `G_W` retains P9-5.1's gradient and rounded-exponent recipe.

`q=(W-W_hat)/(W+W_hat)` remains exact rational through the diagonal block,
current solve and Read-Back multiplication. Its binary64 `contrast` field is
diagnostic: it may round to ±1 while `contrast_exact` remains strictly inside
that interval. Exact zero denominators reject, including with zero baseline;
the exact diagonal 2-norm condition number must meet the declared limit.
The rounded result must satisfy both block and physical Read-Back closure
residuals under the profile's edge-L2 tolerances. Finite signed gains outside
the sufficient uniform region are checked on their actual regular domain;
they are not clipped into that sufficient region.

Structural lowering uses the admitted `H1_form` solve with its conditioning
and residual checks. Physical and structural coordinates stay distinct even
when dense Hodge coupling gives a loop a nonzero structural form and zero
physical read flux.

The log writer's recipe is
`grcv4-a-log-interpolation-decimal120-binary64-v1`: exact binary64 inputs,
120-digit half-even Decimal ratio/log/exp arithmetic, one final binary64
rounding per retained weight. Its fresh local Decimal context explicitly pins
precision, rounding, exponent bounds (`±999999`), capitals (`1`), clamp (`0`), initially clear flags,
and traps (`InvalidOperation`, `DivisionByZero`, `Overflow` only). Expected
rounding signals do not trap; caller current/default contexts are untouched.
It forms the increment around the nearer endpoint, avoiding premature binary64 `dt/tau` division or `1-exp(-dt/tau)`
cancellation. Identical endpoints and zero duration preserve the exact input.
For `dt/tau >= 1000`, returning the exact target is justified by binary64
resolution: the remaining log displacement is below `1455 exp(-1000)`, far
below half an ULP relative to any positive binary64 endpoint. This is a
declared finite-precision recipe, not a universal correct-rounding theorem.
There is no post-write floor clamp; below-floor positive history remains lawful.
