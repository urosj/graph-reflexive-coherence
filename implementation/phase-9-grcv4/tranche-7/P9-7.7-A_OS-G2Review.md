### Ordered-endpoint applicability decision

The specification says that endpoint support does not prove crossing support;
the initializer supplement applies when its exact policy is selected. Therefore:

- The nomination's supplied-history policy does not promise incoming C→A
  construction. The actual well-formed request rejects at admission without
  publication. This is the required negative boundary for that unavailable
  policy, **not** a successful C→A operation or a waiver of the producer law.
- Positive C→A has accepted evidence for the separately identified,
  initializer-enabled target. That profile needs its own complete product
  before it can be proposed as additional G2 support. Its evidence is not
  transferred to this nomination.
- Nominated A_OS→A_CI, A_OS→C_OS, A_OS→A_PC and A_PC→nominated A_OS retain
  their actual positive pairs and finite inputs. Their target/source declarations
  are local crossing fixtures, not additional advertised profiles. In particular,
  the C_OS crossing target is not the already accepted dyadic C_OS nomination.
- PC↔CI+PC cannot have this nonpersistent OS nomination as either endpoint.
  Their separate accepted witnesses document the generic class and earn no
  A_OS execution credit. They are outside this profile's crossing population,
  rather than omitted mandatory A_OS cases.
- The mapped event has a nominated A_OS source and a distinct selected A_OS
  initializer target. Its resource/history map and reset are fully tested;
  this does not claim reconstruction **into** the supplied-history nomination.

All seven classes have explicit dispositions. No unknown ordered pair is
promoted, and no positive class applicable to the declared population is
replaced by a negative. A future request for broader crossing support must
review those endpoints and operations explicitly; accepting this profile is
not sufficient. This is the same distinction between nominated support and
separate crossing vectors used in the accepted C_OS G2 review.
