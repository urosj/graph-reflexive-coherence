## Numerical findings and the next bounded experiment

The existing `ChargeEvaluation` explicitly distinguishes its rounded diagnostic
from the exact sum of stored coordinates. The retained native-kernel probe has
two vertices with C=(1,2), h=1 and edge current 2^-53. The represented write is
(1-2^-53,2). Its charge diagnostic is 3 with residual zero and admission true,
but its exact charge is 3-2^-53. This is a continuity counterexample, not a
selected C_OS solver or a claim that the spark trajectory has failed.

There is also an input issue: independently rounding the accepted C_OS current
prestate to binary64 changes its exact charge from 21 by **2^-50**. Its reset
prestate happens to retain exact charge, which is not a preservation theorem.
Consequently, changing only the evaluator and enabling a detector cannot satisfy
the accepted exact-charge contract. FLINT internal solves alone do not fix
authoritative input storage or per-vertex output rounding.

The certificate calculation reconstructs S and U from the retained exact basis
and radii, checks U S=I and every column of S has zero sum, and uses the retained
C_OS strict image margin. For a **charge-tangent** componentwise error bound e,

`||U deltaC||inf <= ||U||inf e`, with `||U||inf ≈ 2.99353e6`.

The retained margin lower bound is approximately 0.00457383, giving the sufficient
condition **e < 1.52791e-9** for this image-margin test. Exact rational operands
and the threshold are retained in the verification record. This is neither a
bound on the actual native full-step error nor permission to use this tolerance
in a solver. Diagnostic agreement, source/entry control and OS admission remain
separate. No part of the estimate permits an error normal to the charge slice.

Next, .2 should compare explicit conservative resource/continuity encodings on
the exact source and a bounded ordinary-step example, including allocation and
checkpoint round-trip. Prefer preserving exact charge by construction, with
lossless rational operands and certified numerical views, over a new drift
theorem. Select and document that representation before connecting the quartic
owner. Account for any rounding of flux/coordinates in the full-step bound; do
not silently correct one resource or treat a display float as exact authority.
This experiment must determine the actual new state/codec contract and a finite
precision/error budget; neither a policy ID nor that implementation is claimed
complete here. It requires no long trajectory or new attraction search.

## Lifecycle ownership and hierarchy boundary

`grc_9_v4_owner._admit_state` currently rejects non-null child stabilization.
Replace that blanket restriction only for a fully admitted new completion
configuration. Keep recognition, executable implementation and reviewed support
separate. The new `completion_policy` must not be inferred from a historical
untyped `spark.child_stabilization` mapping.

Extend the branch's immutable publication with the active candidate/window and
consumed-event/child records, including their explicit snapshot representation.
Bind an occurrence to the actual successful event receipt and ancestry, not to
a research hash, proof ID or window number. Failed preparation/publication
changes neither scientific state nor progress/gain. Reset/rebase invalidates the
candidate without forgetting an already consumed occurrence. Replay/import and
duplicate restore prior consumption; a replacement certificate cannot mint gain.
Also pressure duplication before completion followed by completion in both
copies: copied consumed flags alone do not establish once-only accounting for
that case. The .3 owner/checkpoint contract must define occurrence reconciliation
at the sole accounting boundary; a research digest or independent local counter
cannot supply it. No global cross-owner uniqueness is claimed by this mapping.

The base window contract requires completion and canonical new-child insertion
in the same transaction. This **minimal event-to-child registration is included**
in .3; broad hierarchy evolution, causal subsystem semantics and numeric
abundance remain deferred. Do not omit a required completion record merely
because the old parent row bundled completion with general hierarchy. The
surviving parent is not registered as a new child.
