## Implemented operation

[CandidateCOSOperation](../../../src/pygrc/models/grc_v4_lifecycle.py) admits a
fresh C_OS reference/current/reset/charge tuple and empty ledger. It is an
internal ordinary-operation receiver, not the complete public GRCV4 facade or a
snapshot importer. Each call binds its actual decoded request, current resource,
reference geometry, clock, reset and ledger. It accepts constant-zero context,
closed internal resource evolution and the already implemented numerical policies.

A positive operation executes the actual reference predictor, one geometry
update, fresh complete corrector, selector-path and split admission, one corrector
continuity write, charge gate and final-C reconstruction at consumed geometry.
It then **reconstructs final C at reference geometry before commit**. The latter
is a read-only complete-poststate check under `final_reconstruction`, not a second
OS pass, geometry update, residual or continuity evaluation. Only C, declared
clock/index and receipt ledger change; corrected geometry, currents, selectors
and residuals have no next-beat authority.

Successful receipts, target identities, immutable target state and result binding
are all constructed before one lifecycle-state pointer is published. No fallible
validation, serialization or callback follows that publication. Calls are serialized.
Failures retain the entire old tuple and ledger; their emitted failure receipt is
not appended to persistent history. Unexpected exceptions before publication also
leave the old tuple intact and propagate. This is not hostile-interpreter or crash/
durable-storage atomicity.

Zero duration re-admits state/reset but performs no OS pass, continuity or clock
advance. It is a successful operation with a receipt delta: scientific identity
stays fixed while lifecycle identity changes. Positive subnormal duration remains
positive even if resource/time round unchanged; the index advances once. The
inherited clock convention is one binary64 `time + dt` and one safe-integer index
increment for positive duration. Overflow rejects at admission. This leaf moves
maximum-safe-index overflow ahead of numerical work so its receipt does not claim
a solver failure. Negative duration rejects before any solver.

## Receipt conventions and failure meaning

The universal frozen ReceiptCore requires resource-transform and history-bundle
identities even for an ordinary step. This owner makes the following explicit,
reviewable local conventions without changing the frozen schema:

- `resource_transform_digest` identifies identity vertex relocation with zero
  **event** increment (`identity_resource_transport_v1`). It does not represent
  the nonlinear continuity map. Actual resource evolution is bound by the source/
  target state and authoritative digests. No topology/resource event occurs here.
- The candidate history channel is `rederived` using the profile's declared
  history policy; the absent carrier channel is `not_applicable` with
  `no_persistent_carrier_v1`. No history loss or transport is invented.
- Receipts are emitted in local order: step commit, charge, candidate disposition,
  carrier disposition. The previous actual step-commit receipt is their sole
  parent, or there is no parent for a fresh owner. This makes consecutive local
  ordinary commits reconstructible. This is a provisional local schema-filling
  convention, not a resolved receipt-lineage or imported parent-DAG contract.
- `actual_charge_delta` is the once-rounded difference of the declared reduced
  binary64 charges. It is neither an exact-sum conservation assertion nor a
  corrective resource increment. Receipt IDs precede the commit ID, which precedes
  envelopes and target lifecycle identity, preserving the acyclic hash contract.

These ordinary conventions are implementation choices subject to this leaf's
review, not additional equations attributed to the paper or tool. This leaf binds
ordinary receipt content, ordering and local reconstructibility. The later lineage
owner may preserve or revise the parent convention and resulting receipt identities
when settling historical versus intra-commit parent scope.

Numerical causes now carry typed C solver dispositions through existing OS stage
wrapping. Failure mapping does not parse message text. Nonfinite arithmetic is
distinct from a finite geometry-domain failure. Nonfinite conditioning-decomposition
output is classified as nonfinite, not finite conditioning-margin loss; nonfinite
reset readmission retains that cause with a null pre-solver disposition. Explicit
backend/reset fault controls pressure both branches. A rejected numerical residual means
that this declared algorithm produced no numerically admitted root; it does not
prove the exact constitutive equation has no mathematical solution. After a valid
corrector, typed resource, charge and final-reconstruction failures keep
`solver_disposition=valid_root`; pre-solver admission failures keep it null.
Receipt construction, identity validation, lifecycle/result construction and
result binding are outside the semantic handler. Programmer `ValueError`,
`V4IdentityError` and `RuntimeError` propagate unchanged, with no publication.
The OS, continuity and charge wrappers likewise catch only typed scientific causes;
finite positivity loss and charge-domain loss have explicit originating types.
