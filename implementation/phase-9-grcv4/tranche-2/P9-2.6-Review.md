## What is verified

candidate/realization pairs, two solver parameterizations each) through
resolution and canonical reconstruction. Complete IDs, family labels and
marginals all remain rejected by executable lookup. Resolution cannot populate
the empty support set. Existing tests retain malformed/forged profile, typed
numeric equality, wide-map traversal and reference-identity pressure.

The recursive ownership check traverses profile, current/reset lifecycle,
request, migration, event, result and receipt storage. Mutable configuration
and returned common events cannot alias retained values; the common field
protocols work without inheriting mutable legacy dataclasses or duplicating
`Q_target`. Existing array/view, history/carrier, nested payload and receipt
regressions remain part of the full foundation suite. This is normal Python
value immutability, not protection against hostile `object.__setattr__`, native
memory writes, unsupported reinitialization or untrusted pickle.

Zero, smallest positive subnormal and maximum finite binary64 duration produce
only strict request records. They cannot complete through the negative-prefix
adapter or make an unresolved context/profile executable. These are positive
characterizations of the prefix's **limited** contract, not claims of full
graph/domain admission. P9-4.4/P9-4.5/P9-4.7a must carry them into real consumers.
Current no-facade/export assertions are stage-local and must evolve when a real
facade is authorized; adding a placeholder would not verify its contract.

## Installed distributions

The hardened opt-in test checks wheel/sdist archive membership and exact
production bytes, then creates four fresh installations: wheel/sdist ×
absent/full V4 extra. Each runs three fresh processes (legacy-first,
foundation-first, failure followed by legacy use). Partial RFC8785-only and
validator-only cells follow separately. The legacy-first and failure-then-legacy
labels repeat the same import order; both compare older behavior before and
after V4 failures. They are repeat executions, not three distinct import orders.
Actual dependencies, module/resource
origins and hashes are recorded; every installed provider must resolve inside
that environment and match the recorded source. No bootstrap patch, codec
substitute, test/investigation import or repository path injection is used.

Full installations exercise published identities, large-coefficient/count
declarations, canonical requests, the real negative result/receipt with
independent expected data, successful content reconstruction, ownership,
unsupported lookup and compact representation counterexamples. Successful
content is not executed. Five older concrete families exercise construction,
steps/run, signatures, save/load, restoration independence, reset, exceptions,
observables and capabilities before/after V4 use and across installations.
These deterministic minimal-state checks complement the unchanged legacy
regressions; they do not prove V4 disabled-delegate parity. The legacy
capability registry retains its separate four-family roster.

Four assets are individually removed or changed by one byte, with both warm
reads and fresh processes required to reject them. Two further controls change
the release or reindex replacement local assets (including a recomputed release
preimage/ID and detached manifest hash); neither satisfies code-bound trust
anchors. Valid neighboring assets cannot rescue a missing installed file.
Restoration and returned-schema isolation also work. `pip check` runs in the
four main cells. Archive hashes identify tested containers, not a promise of
byte-identical later builds; their checked payloads/build inputs are bound.
Scratch installations are disposable.
