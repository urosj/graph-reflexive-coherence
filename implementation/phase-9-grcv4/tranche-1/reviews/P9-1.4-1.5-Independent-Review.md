## 3. P9-1.4: what passes

### 3.1 A genuine first vertical slice

The dependency register does not equate C_OS dynamics with complete conformance. It requires the early snapshot/reset/replay slice, positive same-graph migration between distinct admitted C_OS identities, a positive affine mapped event, reference-map readmission, failure cases, and receipt/duplication reconciliation before P9-4.8 and G2[C_OS].

This is materially stronger than helper-level or one-step acceptance. The separate A_OS follow-on and singleton GRC9V4[C_OS] route are explicit and do not require unrelated realizations.

**Sources:** S1 `/dependency_edges`, `/child_iterations`; S2 “Exact early path and branch conditions.”

### 3.2 Complete catalog projection without pretending it is a concrete suite

All ten profile rows contain the correct base catalog case sets. The record correctly labels them `not_executed_not_a_concrete_fixture_product` and explains that parameters, domains, graphs, context, identity, and semantic-negative cases add further dimensions.

The seed identity is explicitly not reused with a different graph or reference map. The nine-edge profile-identity example, the three-node algebra witness, and the mapped-event source/target remain separate fixtures.

**Sources:** S1 `/profiles`, `/first_slice_identity_policy`; S2 “Support rows.”

### 3.3 Migration evidence is classified correctly

A negative rejection of an unsupported target does not count as a positive migration. An unavailable source is not instantiated merely to manufacture a crossing result. Same-family migration still requires distinct exact identities and actual current/reset transport.

The seven classes remain visible, with exact subset applicability to be reviewed at G2 rather than silently waived. This is an appropriate hold policy, not an instruction to implement all ten families to make the first gate pass.

**Sources:** S1 `/migration_classes`; S2 “Migration applicability and gate holds.”

### 3.4 Independent specialization scope

C_OS mechanics, C_PC carrier work, and Candidate A expansion have distinct prerequisites. D30/D31/D45/D52 are the accepted-vector cases; D37/D44 are additional probes, not replacements. The C_OS carrier disposition remains `not_applicable`.

**Sources:** S1 `/specialization_policy`; S2 “GRC9V4 follow-on.”

## 4. P9-1.4 correction A: separate optional completion from mandatory crossings

**Classification:** material scheduling/requirement clarification; not a scientific defect.
**Affected scope:** the later specialization acceptance path, not the C_OS-first path.

### Exact evidence

S1 currently contains:

```text
P9-8.6 -> P9-9.1 -> P9-9.4 -> P9-9.6
```

Here P9-9.1 combines child stabilization/completed spark/hierarchy with specialization lifecycle work. S2 says that full GRC9V4 support also needs child/lifecycle evidence. S1 `/specialization_policy/full_conformance` similarly includes child cases without an explicit capability condition.

However, the release-bound GRC9V4 specification, “Capabilities,” makes `completed_spark` and `hierarchy_tracking` separately advertised optional capabilities. They require the exact stabilization policy and their own evidence when advertised. This is distinct from the full-conformance requirement for the four disabled surfaces of each supported profile.

### Consequence

A valid decision to leave child completion unadvertised can still block enabled/disabled crossing work and specialization review because the graph requires the entire mixed P9-9.1 parent.

This is not a reason to remove required specialization lifecycle evidence. It is a reason to split that evidence from optional child completion.

### Recommended patch

Keep P9-9.1 as a stable aggregate parent and register children, for example:

```text
P9-9.1a
  child stabilization / completed spark / hierarchy;
  required when those capabilities are in the declared support scope.

P9-9.1b
  mandatory specialization lifecycle, independent of advertising completion.
```

Then bind P9-9.4 to the mandatory lifecycle child and the relevant delegate prerequisites, not automatically to completion. Make P9-9.6's completion prerequisite conditional on the actual capability scope.

Use wording such as:

> Full GRC9V4 conformance requires the applicable generic and specialization contracts and the four disabled surfaces for every advertised profile. Child-stabilization and hierarchy evidence is additionally required when those optional capabilities are advertised. All unselected planned work remains visible.

The project is free to require completed spark before a particular Phase 9 handoff. If that is intended, record it as a **stronger project-chosen handoff target**, not as a requirement imposed by the frozen specification or a dependency of every enabled/disabled crossing.

### Pressure checks for the planning verifier

- Completion disabled: required lifecycle and delegate evidence can reconcile without a fake completed-spark result.
- Completion enabled but its evidence absent: completion capability and any handoff explicitly requiring it remain held.
- Completion disabled must not waive mechanical trigger, topology, charge, reset, receipt, or legacy compatibility obligations.

## 5. P9-1.4 correction B: normalize the realization field

**Classification:** small machine-record naming correction.

S1 uses:

```json
{"profile_family": "A_CI_PC", "realization": "CI_PC"}
{"profile_family": "C_CI_PC", "realization": "CI_PC"}
```

The release-bound schema at `/$defs/profile_identity_payload/properties/realization` uses `CI+PC`. Its family discriminators pair `A_CI_PC` and `C_CI_PC` with `realization = "CI+PC"`.

Prefer:

```json
{"profile_family": "A_CI_PC", "realization": "CI+PC"}
{"profile_family": "C_CI_PC", "realization": "CI+PC"}
```

Keep the family names, iteration IDs, filenames, and any deliberately internal source-group key `CI_PC` unchanged. If the current field is intentionally a planning-only key, rename it `realization_key` and add an explicit conversion instead. Do not allow an unlabelled planning alias to become the serialized runtime value.

Add a source-backed family/candidate/realization comparison to the review checker. A roster count alone would not catch this mismatch.

## 6. P9-1.5: ownership assessment

### 6.1 The single commit owner is correct

The provisional complete-step evaluator and the authoritative lifecycle commit are distinct. Candidate evaluation and realization code cannot commit; lifecycle owns the complete admitted tuple, rollback, reset, and receipts.

This avoids a partial-success path where current, resource, or carrier state becomes authoritative before target admission finishes.

**Sources:** S3 `/modules` entries `grc_v4_step` and `grc_v4_lifecycle`; S4 “Module and API ownership.”

### 6.2 Candidate injection and generic/specialized separation are appropriate

The dependency graph is acyclic. Generic modules do not depend on GRC9 modules. Realizations consume candidate evaluators through interfaces, so an unimplemented A or RG2b provider need not prevent C_OS import or testing.

At implementation time, the composition point that supplies those providers must be explicit, but this review need not prewrite every protocol or factory signature. Absence of a provider must reject that exact requested profile; it must not select a sibling or silently substitute another realization.

**Sources:** S3 `/modules`, `/dependency_policy`; S4 “Module and API ownership.”

### 6.3 Legacy boundaries are appropriately narrow

The 135-path register separates 133 frozen legacy references from two proposed additive integration targets: `src/pygrc/models/__init__.py` and `pyproject.toml`. Current-tree checking is explicitly diagnostic for this review, not a permanent blanket prohibition on subsequently authorized V4 additions.

The V4 codec and standalone immutable records are justified local adaptations; the record does not propose mutating old dataclass storage, old JSON encoding, or legacy capability behavior merely to accommodate V4.

**Sources:** S3 `/legacy_bindings`, `/legacy_binding_policy`, `/proposed_integration_paths`; S4 “Integration boundaries found in existing code.”

### 6.4 Oracle and evidence separation is strong

Production is forbidden from importing test oracles. The frozen vector generator is not an allocator or numerical oracle implementation. Runtime results bind code, complete model/profile identity, exact fixture inputs and expected values, numerical comparison rules, receipts, and both scientific and lifecycle states.

Comparing complete payloads in addition to digests is especially valuable: the same defective serializer cannot conceal both sides of a rollback error merely by producing matching incomplete hashes.

Existing core/model baseline tests are correctly labelled as legacy regression evidence, not V4 conformance.

**Sources:** S3 `/oracle_policy`, `/retention_policy`, `/baseline_test_results`; S4 “Independent oracle and test boundaries,” “Artifact retention.”

## 7. P9-1.5 follow-through items to register, not complete now

These are implementation pressure assignments. They do not require redesigning the seventeen-module decomposition or withholding the ownership review until runtime exists.

### 7.1 Installed-package access to normative assets

The review proposes an optional V4 dependency extra and a codec that validates release-bound schemas, but does not explicitly assign how those schemas and required identity metadata are obtained from an installed distribution.

**Owner:** `grc_v4_codec` with the reviewed `pyproject.toml` integration.
**Suggested leaves:** P9-2.2 and P9-2.6.

Record a concrete resource-loading/packaging plan. Test a clean installed distribution with no source checkout, no `tests/`, and no `implementation/` directories. The codec must not silently read ambient repository files, fetch a mutable remote schema, or import the vector builder.

Also test legacy imports without the V4 extra, V4 imports with it, and a typed missing-dependency failure for a V4 request without it. This is future implementation evidence, not a claim about package versions presently installed.

### 7.2 Make reuse/adaptation decisions explicit

The legacy hash register says what must remain intact, not which functions or backend implementations will actually be reused.

**Suggested leaves:** P9-2.1, P9-2.6, P9-3.1.

A short per-component disposition is enough:

```text
use unchanged / wrap read-only / implement V4-local replacement / not consumed
```

Bind the chosen symbol and the reason to its contract. The codec, immutable authority model, and corrected expansion already have explicit reasons for V4-local implementation. Graph storage/lookup and utility reuse should be decided by the same rule, not by a target reuse percentage.

No shared refactor is justified merely by code similarity. No clean V4 implementation is rejected merely because it is new code.

### 7.3 Retain the distinct tau_C control

The oracle summary names kappa-M, chi, and zeta zero controls. The accepted D11-C resolution additionally specifies the distinct `tau_C = 0` boundary: explicit resolvent filtering disappears, while the H_M-conditioned direct baseline need not disappear.

**Suggested leaf:** P9-4.3, as supplemental evidence linked to the frozen source, not an edit to the frozen catalog.

Exercise this on a nontrivial retained-Hodge case. Do not infer it from a run with kappa-M also zero or from an identity-Hodge example where the direct path is unobservable.

### 7.4 Bind numerical reproducibility to the promised scope

A proposed dense binary64 backend is a reasonable review choice, not a numerical-conformance result. The exact contract comparison policy controls which outputs require exact bytes and which permit declared numerical tolerances.

**Suggested leaves:** P9-2.5, P9-3.4, P9-4.5.

Record the backend/environment with the run and test nonidentity SPD cases, near-but-admitted selector/current conditioning boundaries, and repeated eigenvalues within a selected cluster with a strict gap at its boundary. Do not replace a projector contract with eigenvector-sign identity. Do not silently add damping, regularization, a pseudoinverse, solver fallback, or charge repair to obtain a passing result.

### 7.5 Preserve current and reset as different test subjects

**Suggested leaves:** the registered C_OS/A_OS lifecycle children.

Start from different live and reset states, then exercise migration or an affine event. Check both reconstructed targets, the persistent ledger, the operation receipt delta, and reset-after-operation. A test with identical current/reset inputs cannot expose a mapper that only transforms one of them.

This strengthens the already correct whole-lifecycle policy without introducing new state or changing its equations.

## 8. Pressure guidance for P9-1.6–1.8

The existing seven review-data probes are reported, not independently re-executed here. For the successor verification work, preserve their limited role and add pressure at the authority/composition boundary:

1. A green architecture review with `runtime_authorized = false` must never unlock `src/`, `tests/`, or dependency changes.
2. Valid planning additions must not be rejected merely because the historical release auditor had an older mutation envelope; the historical checks themselves remain intact.
3. Removing a required lifecycle child, forging an alias, or referring to a nonexistent dependency must hold the affected acceptance gate, even if profile/group counts remain unchanged.
4. Leaving A/PC/RG2b unsupported must not block C_OS. Conversely, advertising an unexecuted exact identity must fail even when its family appears in the planned roster.
5. Optional completion must neither become a universal prerequisite nor be advertised without its own required evidence.
6. Altered frozen bytes, stale predecessor hashes, contradictory status fields, and permitted-path names containing unauthorized content must not gain acceptance merely from matching an expected filename or a passing checker string.

These are successor-verification checks, not requests to run V4 dynamics in Tranche 1.
