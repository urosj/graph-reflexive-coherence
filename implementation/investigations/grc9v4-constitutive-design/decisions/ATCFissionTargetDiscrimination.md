# CAN-F2 — initial endowment versus dynamic support

**Status:** bounded research follow-up, not scientific acceptance. The
[F2 proposal](./ATCSourceFissionProposal.md), evaluator and retained onset result
are unchanged. The review supports continuing the hypothesis, not accepting
the neighbor-resource benchmark as a general physical-support law.

## 1. Scientific disposition of the review

F2 permits physical fission of a currently convergent parent when its fixed
activity allocation initially endows each child above its continuing exterior
neighbor. This is a new constitutive choice about growth, not a capacity theorem,
an inherited obligation to divide, or CAN-B's structural-mode criterion.

The rounded guard has a useful exact consequence on its admitted represented
resource inputs. Monotonicity of rounding, which fixes a represented neighbor,
gives

$$
\operatorname{RN}_{64}(\lambda_i C_v)>C_{n_i}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_i C_v>C_{n_i}.
$$

Adding the inequalities, with complementary shares, proves

$$
C_v>C_{n_0}+C_{n_1}.
$$

Thus the parent holds more than half the local three-vertex resource. This is
**relative initial endowment**, not a dynamically determined vertex capacity.
Two inward participations establish instantaneous accumulation at the declared
read, not sustained accumulation or its history. Common positive scaling of the
currents preserves the ideal activity ratio; represented arithmetic must still
preserve that ratio and its signs. F2 does not separately threshold their size.
The K0 OS reference read is not generally the ordinary step's consumed current;
the zero-channel control below is a case where the distinction disappears.

The review's favorable symmetric argument and asymmetric counterexamples must
be retained together. Neither predicate success nor target admission proves
that children continue to accumulate, preserve external inflow, form stable
organizations, or relieve a burden. The original proposal already declined
those guarantees; this follow-up tests and limits its possible interpretation,
rather than attributing a contradicted theorem to it.
