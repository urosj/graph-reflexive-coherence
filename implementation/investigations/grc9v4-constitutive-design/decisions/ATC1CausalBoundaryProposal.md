## 2. Proposed physical input: a narrower, explicit first law

For an already admitted source publication s, let

$$
\sigma_0(s)=(\mathcal G,p,R,U,X_{\mathrm{current}}).
$$

R means the decoded, complete reference package: pairings, structural base,
stable-edge references and any declared differential/backend recipe. U means
the declared context contract and its actual value, not an arbitrary label.
The graph/reference/profile consistency checks remain prerequisites. A hash
without its reconstructible content is not a numerical operand.

The dynamic coordinate is exactly one of C, (C,W), (C,Z), (C,W,Z), according
to the candidate and realization. Derived T_C, current, Hodge, structural
sources, roots, sections and solver caches are recomputed, not independent
inputs. Incoming W is fixed during a current read; present Z is fixed during
a persistent/composite read.

K0 excludes reset, charge target, receipt/archive history, operation IDs,
telemetry, step index, accumulated time, request duration and failed-attempt
counts from physical resolution. Its initial context envelope is the existing
constant-zero/no-external-input runtime domain. This is intentionally narrower
than Draft 8's permission to use already physical time/context. A time-varying
forcing or clock-dependent law must declare a different causal projection.

Static duration parameters inside a profile's admitted RG completion or
carrier law remain part of p. Excluding the latest requested duration does
not remove those parameters. The latest positive request still changes
ordinary evolution and therefore may change the resulting physical input.

For admitted sources s and s' with equal projections, a total deterministic
evaluation on its declared domain must satisfy

$$
\sigma_0(s)=\sigma_0(s')
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Gen}_{\theta}(\sigma_0(s))
=\operatorname{Gen}_{\theta}(\sigma_0(s')).
$$

This follows by function substitution; it is not an empirical theorem about
an unimplemented evaluator. Under relabeling/signed-coordinate actions the
comparison is instead equality of physical correspondence classes, with all
reference/profile operands transformed. IDs may locate coordinates and bind
provenance, but may not select a physical winner through lexicographic order,
hash bits or a pseudo-random seed derived from the source identity.

## 3. Witness stage fixed by the realization

The K0 witness is reconstructed from the *committed ordinary poststate*, not
the current that the preceding step consumed. Bind one stage per realization:

| Products | K0 present-state read | Existing numerical owner / limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A_OS, C_OS | Candidate current at reference Hodge. | `CandidateACurrent` / `CandidateCCurrent`; no fresh OS predictor/corrector pass. |
| A_CI, C_CI | Fresh admitted joint current/geometry root. | `CandidateCIRoot`; no previous-root seed or stale selector. |
| A_PC, C_PC | Rebuild H from present committed Z, then read the candidate chain. | `carrier_geometry`, `CandidatePCRead`; no carrier writer. |
| A_CI_PC, C_CI_PC | Fresh coupled root with present Z fixed and immediate source in the effective structural input. | `CandidateCIRoot`; do not double-count the immediate source in a later writer. |
| A_RG2b, C_RG2b | Reconstruct the section and native candidate current on the state-admission domain K. | `CandidateRG2bSection`, then candidate current; no claim of a classical derivative or next-step eligibility in K_minus. |

These are proposed adapter semantics tied to actual numerical owners, not
ten newly supported ATC products. Each concrete law still owes complete
profile/domain applicability and native evidence in ATC-2.

The lifecycle helper `_readmit_state` shows the existing state/next-step
distinction, but also validates reset. It is not automatically a current-only
physical-witness API. The future adapter must separate already-admitted source
validation from witness computation and demonstrate that excluded reset and
administrative data cannot affect physical output or computational budget.
Do not change the authoritative reset to current to manufacture admission.

Ordinary steps, zero-duration lifecycle operations and pure numerical reads
are different operations. Calling `step_v4(dt=0)` is not an implementation of
the witness: it can append a successful receipt and, for RG2b, imposes the
ordinary entry contract. Witness computation performs no continuity update,
W/Z writer or publication. Optional fresh OS-pass and historical-consumed
witnesses are retained alternatives, not automatic fallbacks.

For basic OS reference Hodge, distinct nonadjacent vertices have zero
off-diagonal entry in B H B^T. Thus K0 gives CAN-A no positive nonedge-coupling
signal in those OS cells. That is a valid limitation, not a reason to switch
stages to obtain a desired event. The more general off-diagonal-H example in
Draft 8 proves an algebraic possibility, not ordinary-runtime reachability.

## 4. Identity, resolution, construction and binding

An enabled identity must bind the base profile and complete theta before
evaluation. Theta includes K0's projection/stage/schedule, one selected law,
exact guard and score, resolution/arithmetic/work-budget recipes, graph and
resource constructors, target reference/context transformations, W/Z policies
and required target assertions. Missing choices reject at static policy
admission *before* ordinary evolution; they are not runtime callbacks.
`ATC-K0` by itself is not a complete-profile identity or an accepted schema.

For the first comparison, one law is enabled per exact policy. No across-law
ranking or simultaneous batch semantics is selected. Exact physical ambiguity
returns `unresolved`; numerical inability to certify returns `uncertified`
with the originating stage/reason. `no_event` requires sufficient evidence
that the chosen law has no active event. A missing evaluator, insufficient
regularity, work-budget exhaustion or solver failure is not a zero witness.
Work limits must count deterministic work, not elapsed wall time; an external
timeout/cancellation is an interruption, not a scientific guard outcome.

Once resolved, the prescription determines the entire target construction.
There is no runner-up, chart enlargement, clipping, alternate history policy
or weakened certificate after construction/admission failure. CAN-C's exact
score and target construction remain ATC-2 work, so K0 does not claim that
CAN-C is already executable.

Binding then consumes the real publication:

$$
e_{\mathrm{wire}}=\operatorname{Bind}(s_1,e_{\mathrm{phys}}).
$$

It supplies source/reset identities, existing event preimages and naming after
physical resolution. The same resource map is applied independently to real
current and reset; initializer invocations and target readmission remain
role-bound. Excluded reset/charge data may cause rejection of this selected
event without choosing another physical locus.

Physical equality does not imply wire, receipt or target equality. Existing
event IDs need not contain receipt history; the wrapper's freshness check must
separately bind the full publication/previous successful primary. It must
detect a changed publication even when scientific coordinates or clock values
coincide. Output evidence never becomes its own static identity input.

## 5. Two transactions under one serialized evolution operation

K0 proposes one ATC evolution operation with these boundaries:

1. Validate the selected complete static policy and ordinary request, then
   enter the lifecycle owner's serialized critical section. Capture s0 and
   check every source-dependent policy/request binding inside that section;
   preliminary validation outside it cannot authorize a stale publication.
2. Run the unchanged ordinary transaction. Failure/exception before its commit
   leaves s0 and performs no ATC evaluation.
3. After a successful ordinary commit s1, evaluate once **iff the accepted
   request duration is strictly positive**. Do not use `time_after > time_before`
   as the test: positive binary64 durations can leave the rounded clock equal.
4. Reconstruct the witness and resolve/build/bind the event from s1. No event,
   unresolved, uncertified or construction rejection leaves s1 unchanged.
5. Attempt the existing whole-lifecycle event transaction. Success publishes
   s2; rejection or exception before event publication preserves s1. Event
   time and ordinary step index do not advance again.

| Ordinary result | ATC/event result | Committed publication and ledger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy/request invalid or ordinary failed | not attempted | s0; no successful ordinary/event group |
| Successful zero-duration ordinary operation | not eligible | s1 with its ordinary group only |
| Successful positive beat | no event / unresolved / uncertified | s1 with its ordinary group only |
| Successful positive beat | construction or target rejection | s1; failure evidence is emitted separately, not appended as success |
| Successful positive beat | event committed | s2; ordinary group followed by event group, whose parent is the ordinary primary |
| Successful positive beat | unexpected internal error before event commit | exception propagates with s1 still committed; do not call the ordinary beat failed |

The report must preserve the ordinary success and separately describe the
ATC phase; existing `GRCV4StepResult` is not silently reinterpreted as that
composite result. The future result/archive contract requires a reviewed
successor schema. Do not add unknown fields to current closed payloads.

Serialization is not joint rollback: s1 is a real commit. Conversely, calling
two separately locked public methods permits interleaving and is not enough
to satisfy the proposed operation. The current owner uses a non-reentrant
`Lock`; do not acquire it and then call its locking public methods recursively.
Future integration must use the same owner and non-reentrant internal stages,
not replace the lock or add a second publication owner as a shortcut.

The unselected joint-macro alternative would stage the ordinary result
without publishing it and reject the whole macro back to s0 if the event
failed. That would make ordinary progress conditional on target/reset event
admissibility and require a new combined publication/receipt contract. K0
selects after-commit composition to preserve the existing ordinary success
semantics. Neither schedule follows uniquely from the core texts; they are
different dynamics on failed-event sequences, not interchangeable packaging.

No external mutation callbacks run inside the operation. Binding/publication
still checks the actual source; an asynchronous stale result may never
overwrite a newer publication or roll it back to s1. Such asynchronous
execution is not established by K0's serialized proposal.

## 6. No new memory, hidden retries or crash-recovery promise

The attempt is part of a completed evolution invocation, not a pending event
coordinate. Preview/observation, snapshot/load, duplicate, reset, migration,
representation changes and explicit caller events do not automatically invoke
it. After these operations, a subsequent successful positive ATC beat may do
so under the resulting admitted policy. An incompatible migration rejects
or requires an explicit policy transformation; it cannot silently disable ATC.

Repeated unsuccessful attempts at later positive beats are allowed. No failure
counter, cooldown, refractory period or successful-boundary memo changes the
next physical result. A pure cache must reproduce the same output and cannot
substitute for missing source preimages. At most one attempt per completed
positive invocation proves a finite attempt count for finitely many such
invocations, not a physical-time non-Zeno theorem or a topology-size bound.

Cancellation/crash after the ordinary commit may leave s1 without a completed
ATC phase. K0 does not promise crash-durable exactly-once completion or silently
retry that boundary after loading. Reproducing a completed invocation and
recovering an interrupted one are different guarantees. A pending-attempt
record/recovery protocol would need an explicit successor archive contract
and an argument that it introduces no undeclared physical memory. This
limitation must be visible in any future advertised runtime scope.
