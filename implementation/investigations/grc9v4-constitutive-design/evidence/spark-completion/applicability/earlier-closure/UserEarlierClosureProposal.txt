## 3. Why the larger proof works

### Sharpen the reference contraction bound

The original argument uses an inverse-trace estimate to bound the weakest restoring direction. I replaced that estimate with direct **exact rational matrix inequalities on the charge-tangent space**.

For the retained comparison curvature \(D_0\), exact symmetric elimination verifies

\[
\frac25 P\prec D_0\prec14P

Consequently, the reference resource operator has a \(P\)-norm bound of

\left\|I-\frac1{32}A_0D_0\right\|_P

### Check edge ordering on the actual metric ball

An independent coordinate box enclosing an ellipsoid contains combinations of coordinates that the ellipsoid itself excludes. Requiring every such combination to preserve edge order can make the certificate unnecessarily small.

The weighted row and seed conditions are checked separately over their enclosing box.

## 5. How to get substantially below 600 steps

**The next improvement should be the shape of the proof domain, not another change to the physical law.**

For a ball centered at the same \(c\) in the same resource metric, preserving every edge ordering imposes a geometric radius limit of approximately

\[
r_{\mathrm{order}}\approx2.7583\times10^{-4}.
\]

Beyond that, the ball reaches an edge-equality hyperplane. The new radius \(2\times10^{-4}\) is already reasonably close to this limit. Simply increasing that radius will therefore give diminishing returns.

A better next construction is an **anisotropic full-state domain**: narrow in directions that could reverse a weak edge contrast, but longer in directions along which the actual trajectory is still relaxing. It must still contain a positive-radius neighborhood in every independent C/W direction and satisfy the same strict self-map, contraction and uniform-predicate conditions.

That offers a plausible route toward certifying states closer to the post-365 part of the trajectory. **I have not proved such a domain or a completion time near 365.** It is the next concrete optimization target, not a promised result.

A finite chain of validated neighborhoods leading into the existing trap could also reduce the computational work needed to prove **eventual** completion. But that must not be relabeled **already completed** organization before the trajectory reaches a domain satisfying SC‑1. Prediction and occurrence remain different claims.
