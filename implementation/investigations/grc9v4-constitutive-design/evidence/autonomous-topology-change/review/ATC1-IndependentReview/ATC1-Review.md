## 2. What K0 actually selects

K0 selects a present-state projection, not a universal definition of autonomy:

$$
\sigma_0(s)=(G,p,R,U,x_p),\qquad
x_p\in\{C,(C,W_A),(C,Z_4),(C,W_A,Z_4)\}.
$$

The constant-zero context, time-homogeneous law and absence of new memory are proposed restrictions. The previous consumed current, reset, charge target, receipt/archive history, operation IDs, telemetry, index, accumulated time, request duration and previous failed attempts are not physical generator inputs. Static durations and numerical policies already belonging to the resolved profile remain inputs.

This is internally coherent. In particular, “no new memory” is not “no history”: W and Z remain actual causal state where the base profile provides them. Conversely, the mere availability of a receipt archive does not license a new counter or hysteresis law.

The decisive noninterference requirement is conditional on the same policy and eligible poststate:

$$
\sigma_0(s)=\sigma_0(s')
\implies
\operatorname{Gen}_{\theta}(\sigma_0(s))
=\operatorname{Gen}_{\theta}(\sigma_0(s')),
$$

where equality means the physical prescription, with appropriate representation covariance. Wire identities, freshness and complete target admission remain allowed to depend on the larger publication. They are not part of this equality claim.

The proposed empty concrete-law and exact-profile fields are appropriate for this step. They mean K0 has not yet earned an executable endogeneity claim. ATC-2 is expressly assigned that work; this review does not turn its absence into a defect in the causal-boundary proposal.

### A theorem that should become an explicit applicability disposition

For any diagonal positive reference Hodge,

$$H_{\rm ref}=\operatorname{Diag}(w_e),\qquad K_V=BH_{\rm ref}B^\top,$$

and distinct nonadjacent vertices a,b,

$$
(K_V)_{ab}=\sum_e w_e B_{ae}B_{be}=0.
$$

No incidence column meets both vertices. Parallel edges do not alter the argument; loops have zero incidence. At an isolate, the relevant normalized diagnostic may be undefined rather than zero.

Consequently Draft 8's CAN-R+ / assembled CAN-A guard

$$q_{ab}=|(K_V)_{ab}|/\sqrt{(K_V)_{aa}(K_V)_{bb}}>\theta_q$$

cannot activate on such a read for a nonnegative strict threshold. This is a subfamily theorem, not merely a small-fixture result. The independent checks cover 52 oriented multigraph cases and 260 nonedge entries, plus the dense-H control with entry `-1/4`.

K0's pressure record already contains the diagonal/null observation. It should now be recorded as an explicit limitation of this particular law/read pairing. Do not replace H_ref with an OS-generated H or with H inverse merely to obtain an event: either change would define another read or another witness. Nor should the null result reject all OS topology laws. The later structural/coarsening candidates have different dependencies.

## 4. Three consequences that should be stated with K0 acceptance

### 4.1 Present-state generation does not imply present-state-only acceptance

Two valid source publications can have identical sigma but different reset baselines. They must resolve the same physical event, while the same event can fail whole-target admission for one of them.

The exact countermodel retained by the submission and replayed independently uses

$$T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

With current `(2,2,2)`, the target is `(4,2)`. Reset `(1,2,3)` maps to `(3,3)` and reset `(2,3,1)` maps to `(5,1)`. All source charges are six. A synthetic target upper bound of four distinguishes the reset cases without changing the proposed physical map.

This is not a native chart example. It establishes the logical distinction: reset can veto a target without choosing a different event. The same separation applies to whole-publication freshness and charge-target checks. Reading those fields at admission does not by itself violate K0; allowing them to rank or replace the physical event would.

### 4.2 A current-only rule cannot generally determine an unrecorded temporal crossing

A threshold occupancy and a threshold crossing are different properties. Present value 3/2 with threshold one can follow previous value 1/2 or previous value 3/2. The present input is identical; the crossing labels differ.

More generally, if a desired historical predicate differs between two histories sharing the same sigma, it cannot factor through sigma. This is an information limitation, not a numerical defect. The countermodel does not assert that these particular histories are native-reachable under an accepted GRCV4 law.

Therefore K0 does not generically supply first-crossing detection, refractory time, a failed-attempt counter, or archived-sibling reversion. A special realization might encode the necessary information in existing W/Z, but that sufficiency must be shown. The proposal correctly leaves historical alternatives unselected instead of declaring their debts universally closed.

### 4.3 Request-duration exclusion is a pointwise claim, not timestep invariance

K0's physical generator does not receive the request duration. Its opportunity to act nevertheless occurs after each successful positive ordinary invocation. Those are compatible choices, but they do not imply that one full step and two half-steps yield the same topology history.

An exact synthetic conservative flow `C(t)=(1+t,3-t)` with a guard `C0 >= 3/2` first becomes eligible at time one when sampled after a single unit beat, and at time one-half when sampled after half-beats. No guard reads the duration. The sampling schedule supplies the difference.

K0 should explicitly disclaim beat-subdivision invariance. That does not make it a hidden-chooser design: the sampling contract is openly selected, and GRCV4's ordinary temporal realizations already have duration semantics. It does mean a later claim of physical-time event-rate invariance needs its own proof.

The reported `RN64(1+2^-54)=1` control is correct: positive-beat eligibility must not be inferred from an observed clock change. The twenty-term dyadic sum is also correct, but is only a finite prefix, not a native infinite-Zeno trajectory. Event-per-invocation bounds and physically meaningful rate guarantees remain separate.

## 5. Two transactions: the choice is sound; the result contract still matters

The selected composition is

$$s_0\xrightarrow{\rm ordinary}s_1,\qquad s_1\xrightarrow{\rm event}s_2.$$

Rejecting a missing static policy before the ordinary beat prevents an incomplete declaration from producing partial work. After the ordinary beat commits, a no-event, unresolved, uncertified or rejected event phase leaves s1. A successful event has the ordinary primary as its receipt parent and does not advance the ordinary clock/index again.

Same-owner serialization and nonreentrant internal stages are appropriate requirements. A lock does not merge the two commits into one transaction, and it does not prove crash durability. The proposal explicitly declines durable exactly-once recovery and implicit load retry; those are good ceilings.

The future composite result should distinguish `not_eligible` from an evaluated `no_event`, retain ordinary success when the event phase rejects, and preserve typed scientific failures separately from programmer exceptions. An interruption after the event has committed leaves s2, not s1. This last phase is included in the independent expectation table; it is not claimed to reproduce a submitted runtime bug.

Ordinary observation, load and duplicate must remain non-actuating. The existing `StepResult` must not be retroactively made to describe both commits. The proposal explicitly routes a successor result/archive contract to later work.
