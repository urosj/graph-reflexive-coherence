## 3. The represented-value law is explicit and internally consistent

The scope change from ideal mathematical roots to the finite outputs actually returned by the admitted read is material. ATC-2 declares H, C and J to be the numerical operands of these research laws. Exact rational arithmetic then determines the score and branch decisions. It does not claim an interval enclosure of a hypothetical exact CI root or RG section.

Within that declared scope, the code implements the equations correctly. CAN-A uses a strict threshold on q-squared and a strictly positive separation greater than the specified squared-score gap. CAN-C uses inclusive residual eligibility and strict score dominance. Empty eligibility, unresolved competition and undefined normalization are not silently equated. A sole eligible candidate needs no artificial runner-up.

The squared-score variant is not merely an implementation optimization for a q-gap law. Squaring preserves ranking of nonnegative scores, but an absolute gap after squaring is a different rule. The proposal states this distinction and should preserve it during successor admission.

The independently executed threshold control uses the three-vertex path and H with unit diagonal and off-diagonal x. For the two nonadjacent endpoints, q-squared is x-squared. At x=2^-25 the score equals the 2^-50 threshold and there is no event. Moving x one representable value downward remains no-event; moving it upward resolves the singleton. Strict dominance likewise remains unresolved at exactly the declared gap and resolves only above it. A separate two-eligible merge control produces scores 32/33 and 32/41 and resolves the former.

These results verify the stated finite law. They do not provide solver-error robustness or universal native covariance. The submission keeps those stronger claims open, correctly.

## 4. CAN-A's actual scope is narrower than arbitrary relation birth

The earlier diagonal-reference obstruction is retained correctly. If H is diagonal, nonadjacent vertices have zero off-diagonal entry in B H B^T, because no edge column contains both endpoints. Defined nonedge scores therefore vanish. The selected OS read must not be changed to obtain a positive example while retaining the same K0 identity.

A related conditional limit is worth recording. If H is block diagonal over the graph's connected edge components, then B H B^T is block diagonal over the corresponding vertex components. This law cannot add an edge between those components. More strongly, when H has only shared-vertex edge support, a nonadjacent pair at graph distance greater than two has zero coupling: an incident edge at each endpoint cannot share a vertex. These are conditional algebraic statements. They do not prohibit a different admitted profile with a different H support, and this audit does not assert a universal end-to-end support theorem across every native owner.

## 6. The constructors withstand the inspected arithmetic and graph pressure

The half-edge treatment resolves an important earlier ambiguity. A loop has two endpoint roles; they may remain together or move to separate children. Each old edge identity survives splitting. Merge, by contrast, expressly drops every internal edge of the pair, including its internal loops, while preserving surviving external edge identities and parallel edges. These are stated operation choices, not accidental consequences of using a simple-graph container.

The exact dyadic allocation is a valid resolution of the independently rounded-thirds problem. With N=2^16, k=roundTiesToEven(Nr) and coefficients k/N and (N-k)/N, the represented columns sum exactly to one. Complementing a partition complements its coefficients. The general error bound is 1/(2N). This is a new allocation recipe, not a proof that current magnitude is the unique physical allocation principle.

Exact columns still do not imply exact stored total after componentwise rounding. Splitting the minimum subnormal equally stores zero in each child. The proposal correctly leaves actual numerical charge admission with the existing owner and does not repair a chosen coordinate. Whether such a numerical discrepancy is accepted depends on the declared charge contract; no bitwise conservation theorem follows.

The geometric-mean implementation also matches its claim. Its search brackets the positive real root of the exact product among adjacent binary64 values and compares the product to the exact powered midpoint. Independent checks include minimum subnormal and maximum finite inputs, broad exponents, permutation of the seed set and both midpoint tie parities. This is stronger than a log/exp approximation, but remains a bound on this reference constructor, not final target existence.

The mask preserves symmetry, inherited surviving entries and a Frobenius upper bound. It is not a positive-definiteness theorem. The submitted text explicitly sends final geometry admission back to the native owner. The independent nonzero-K4 controls do not replace that admission.

The A host-position midpoint and duplicated split positions are explicit new target-reference choices. The retained native A merge uses alpha=beta=0 and gamma=0.1. Accordingly, its success does not pressure an active curvature channel merely because the backend preimage exists. That is a scope limit, not a contradiction in the conditional constructor.

## 12. A possible additional bounded theorem, not an accepted transformation

For a fixed selected birth-only law with no external edge-removing operations and the enforced bound m<=32, every successful event increases m by one. Hence successful birth events in such a segment number at most 32-m0. For a merge-only law with no external vertex-increasing operations, every successful event reduces n by one, giving the conservative bound n0-1.

These elementary bounds do not bound failed attempts, guarantee finite accumulated ordinary time, establish mixed-law behavior or prove a general non-Zeno theorem. They could support a very narrow successor claim if useful. This review does not alter any existing debt disposition.
